# DELINEATION OF FRESH GROUNDWATER ZONE IN THE COASTAL AQUIFERS OF BANGLADESH

This research examines the spatial distribution of fresh and saline groundwater across six districts north of the Sundarbans in Bangladesh. It integrates groundwater observations, geospatial analysis, and machine learning to delineate areas with a higher likelihood of fresh groundwater occurrence. The assessment aims to describe the spatial extent of groundwater salinity and support investigation of potential salinity intrusion pathways, providing information for groundwater resource assessment and planning.

Groundwater samples are classified using measured electrical conductivity (EC). Samples with EC below 1,000 µS/cm are assigned to the fresh groundwater class, while samples at or above this threshold are assigned to the nonfresh class. Groundwater level, EC, salinity, and chloride observations are converted into spatial layers using inverse distance weighting with a distance power of 2 and up to 15 neighbouring observations. Terrain attributes are derived from a digital elevation model. The analysis uses WGS 84 / UTM Zone 45N (EPSG:32645), with a nominal raster resolution of approximately 30 m, and excludes the Sundarbans from the final study boundary.

An initial set of 16 spatial predictors is screened for multicollinearity using the Variance Inflation Factor (VIF). The predictor with the highest VIF is removed iteratively until all remaining values are 15 or lower. The final ten predictors are DD (Drainage Density), DIS_Rivers (Distance from Major Rivers), GwCl (Groundwater Chloride), GwEC (Groundwater Electrical Conductivity), GwL (Groundwater Level), GwS (Groundwater Salinity), LULC (Land Use and Land Cover), Soil_Perm (Soil Permeability), TPI (Topographic Position Index), and TRI (Terrain Ruggedness Index). Raster values are extracted at groundwater sampling locations, and samples with missing coordinates, measured EC, or candidate predictor values are excluded.

Five supervised classification models are compared: Support Vector Machine (SVM), Random Forest (RF), Gradient Boosting Machine (GBM), Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), and K Nearest Neighbors (KNN). The sample dataset is divided into approximately 80% training and 20% testing data using a stratified random split with a random seed of 42. Stratification maintains the relative class proportions in both subsets. The Synthetic Minority Oversampling Technique (SMOTE), using five neighbours, balances the training classes. Feature standardisation is fitted to the resampled training data for SVM and KNN and then applied to their testing inputs.

Model performance is evaluated on the held out testing samples using accuracy, ROC curves and area under the ROC curve, precision recall curves, average precision, confusion matrices, sensitivity, specificity, precision, F1 score, Cohen's kappa, and classification reports. Each trained model is applied to valid pixels in the predictor raster stack to generate a georeferenced probability map ranging from 0 to 1. Higher values indicate a higher model estimated probability of groundwater meeting the study's EC criterion for freshness.

Groundwater chemistry layers are interpolated before the training and testing split. Because measured EC also defines the classification target, predictor construction can incorporate information from observations subsequently assigned to the testing set. The resulting scores therefore require caution when interpreting predictive performance at independent locations. The random holdout assessment provides an internal evaluation of the available samples; spatially independent observations are needed to assess performance in new areas.

Install the packages with `python -m pip install -r requirements.txt`. Open the notebook in Jupyter, set `BASE_DIR` to the GIS project directory, and run the cells in order. The required inputs include the study boundary, groundwater sampling records, elevation data, river and land cover layers, soil information, rainfall data, and satellite imagery used to prepare the candidate predictors. The study datasets are not included in this repository. Intermediate spatial layers are prepared under `Python`, and model files, predictor screening tables, evaluation metrics, validation figures, and groundwater probability maps are written to `OUTPUT`.
